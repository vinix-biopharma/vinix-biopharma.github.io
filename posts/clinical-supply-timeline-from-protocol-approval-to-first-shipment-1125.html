Clinical Supply Timeline: From Protocol Approval to First Shipment

Sponsors often underestimate how long it takes to get comparator medicines to the first trial site. Here is a typical sequence when sourcing from India, with the points where delays usually happen.

Weeks 1-2: Forecast and specification

Estimate patients per site and treatment duration. Turn that into a quantity per product, with a buffer for overage. Define the pack format and the minimum remaining shelf life you need.

Weeks 2-4: Supplier selection and documents

Request quotations and the batch documents: manufacturing licence, WHO-GMP certificate and certificate of analysis. Confirm one batch can cover the study.

Weeks 3-8: Import permit

Apply for the trial import permit in each destination country. This step often takes the longest, so start it as soon as the protocol is approved.

Weeks 6-10: Labelling and packing

Clinical labels, kit numbers and any blinding are applied by a GMP packager. Approve label proofs quickly; every revision adds days.

Weeks 8-12: Shipping and receipt

Ship with temperature data loggers where needed. Allow time for customs clearance and site receipt checks.

Common causes of delay

  • Import permit quantities that do not match the shipment
  • Label text approved late
  • Batches with too little remaining shelf life
  • Missing apostille on certificates
