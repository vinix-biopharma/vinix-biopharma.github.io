Hidden Risks in Pharma Contract Manufacturing and How to Reduce Them

Contract manufacturing looks simple on a quotation: a product, a batch size, a delivery date. The real risks are in the details that are not on the quotation. Here are six that importers often meet for the first time after the order is placed.

1. Batch size versus real demand

Plants produce in fixed batches. If the minimum batch is larger than what your market can sell before expiry, you carry stock risk. Ask for the smallest validated batch size and plan the first order around it.

2. Raw material lead time

The production slot may be free, but the active ingredient may take weeks to arrive. Ask whether the plant keeps stock of the API or orders it after your confirmation.

3. Artwork delays

New cartons and foils need printing cylinders and proof approval. One late approval can shift the whole schedule. Name a single person on your side to approve artwork.

4. Remaining shelf life at arrival

Many importers require a minimum remaining shelf life on arrival. Agree on this number in the purchase order, especially for sea freight.

5. Unannounced changes

A change of excipient supplier or packaging material can affect your registration. Your quality agreement should require the manufacturer to inform you before any change.

6. Document gaps at customs

Missing COA, wrong HS code or an invoice that does not match the label can hold a shipment at the port. Ask for draft shipping documents before dispatch.

How to stay in control

  • Get a written quality agreement before the first batch.
  • Ask for a production schedule with clear milestones.
  • Request photos or a video call during the first batch.
  • Review COA and packing list before the goods leave the plant.
